A diffusion forecast can use a fresh correction

Inside AI · AI development · 8 October 2026

Skipping expensive layers saves time. But a forecast from old features can drift as the generated sample changes.

A new preprint by Hanru Bai, Yuanchao Xu and Fengyi Li tests a correction loop for frozen diffusion models. Predict the expensive deep features, compute cheaper shallow features from the current state, then use their prediction error to correct the deep estimate. Occasional full passes refresh it. The denoiser stays frozen; the forecasting operators and correction gains are fitted offline.

The authors report three runs of 10,000 images per dataset. At their four-partial-step schedule, paired Inception-feature MSE fell 19.9% on CIFAR-10 and 11.9% on a ten-class ImageNet subset versus channelwise affine forecasting. Disabling correction in a matched ablation isolated smaller additional gains of 4.54% and 4.67%.

Reported speedups were 1.89× and 1.85× versus full DDIM-50. Those are specific timing results, not a promise for every diffusion model. Reference agreement also isn't the same as better image quality: FID is measured separately. Complete hardware-matched offline fitting cost was not measured.

Skipping layers doesn't have to mean ignoring the current sample. That's what this correction loop tests. These are the authors' preprint results; we haven't reproduced them.
